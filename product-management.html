Product Design

Using research and behavioral insight to create metrics-aware prototypes and clear handoffs for engagement and retention.

Duolingo(Harvard Product Lab Case Study): Retention & Engagement Strategy

Research & Strategy

  • Used cohort analysis (case study) to map where users were dropping off across early milestones
  • Synthesized findings into 3 churn drivers connected to habit formation and early motivation
  • Explored 3 feature directions to address each driver (commitment, momentum, and feedback)

Design & Validation

  • Designed an interactive onboarding flow in Figma with clear “why this step exists” rationale
  • Built prototypes to validate structure, pacing, and feedback loops (not just visuals)
  • Delivered an annotated spec + product brief to support quick engineering iteration

Key Outcomes

  • Metrics-aware: Defined success around 7-day retention, early adoption, and drop-off points
  • Systems thinking: Designed engagement loops that reinforce progress and return behavior
  • Execution-ready: Prototypes and specs structured for a fast build-test cycle

Key Insight & Next Steps

What I learned: The data helped me see where retention risk clustered, but it didn’t fully explain why users disengaged. Behavioral context matters — especially early on, when habits are fragile.

Next steps: I would run lightweight user interviews and compare a few onboarding variations to learn which churn driver matters most. I’d measure impact using 7-day retention and early lesson completion to validate whether the engagement loop is actually working.

JPMorgan(Harvard Product Lab Client): Financial UX & Decision Design

Research & Strategy

  • Led market research and competitive analysis for high-stakes financial workflows
  • Conducted real-time interviews to understand how users make decisions under uncertainty
  • Identified a core friction: decision paralysis driven by overload and low confidence

Design & Validation

  • Designed an experience that reduces cognitive load through progressive disclosure
  • Added confidence-building cues at key decision moments (clarity, reassurance, and structured choices)
  • Iterated quickly based on feedback; translated insights into reusable interaction patterns

Key Outcomes

  • User-centered: Turned interviews into concrete design decisions tied to real behavior
  • Balanced constraints: Considered user trust, clarity, and business requirements together
  • Fast iteration: Moved from research → prototype → refinement in short cycles

Key Insight & Next Steps

What I learned: The problem wasn’t missing information — it was too much information at the wrong time. Users needed help feeling confident as they moved forward, not more options.

Next steps: I would test the redesigned flow against the original to see whether progressive disclosure reduces hesitation. I’d measure completion rate and where users pause or exit to pinpoint the highest-friction decision moments.

Approach

My work centers on understanding user behavior and designing engagement systems that support daily use, habit formation, and emotional connection. I move from research to structured experimentation, focusing on habit formation, decision clarity, and measurable impact.
