Duolingo(Harvard Product Lab Case Study): Retention & Engagement Strategy

Research & Strategy

  • Used cohort analysis (case study) to map where users were dropping off across early milestones
  • Synthesized findings into 3 churn drivers connected to habit formation and early motivation
  • Explored 3 feature directions to address each driver (commitment, momentum, and feedback)

Design & Validation

  • Designed an interactive onboarding flow in Figma with clear “why this step exists” rationale
  • Built prototypes to validate structure, pacing, and feedback loops (not just visuals)
  • Defined the feature structure, interaction flow, and success criteria to support rapid iteration.

Key Outcomes

  • Metrics-aware: Defined success around 7-day retention, early adoption, and drop-off points
  • Systems thinking: Designed engagement loops that reinforce progress and return behavior
  • Execution-ready: Prototypes and specs structured for a fast build-test cycle

Feature Concept: Daily Duo (Friend or Match)

To strengthen early momentum, I explored a lightweight social ritual that makes progress feel shared, not solo. Daily Duo pairs users for a 3-minute daily micro-challenge that builds accountability and emotional reinforcement without adding coordination overhead.

Two pairing options

  • Friend Duo: invite someone you already know for a low-pressure shared routine
  • Match Duo: opt into a comfort-level match (same goals + pace) for users who want community
  • Comfort controls: choose Quiet (no chat), Reactions, or Messages/Voice

Daily experience

  • Shared unlock: a new micro-challenge appears once per day for both users
  • Async friendly: each person completes on their own time; progress syncs when both finish
  • Lightweight responses: quick encouragement (emoji/reaction), optional short voice note
  • Celebrate together: shared streak milestones + “we did it” moments

Habit loop design

  • Trigger: coordinated notification (“Your Duo challenge is ready”)
  • Action: 3-minute micro-lesson/mini challenge built around a shared prompt
  • Reward: shared streak + visible progress + partner acknowledgement
  • Reinforcement: small social signals make return behavior feel personal

What I’d Measure

  • 7-day retention: lift for Daily Duo vs. solo onboarding
  • Next-day return: % returning within 24 hours after first Duo completion
  • Duo completion rate: how often both users finish the daily challenge
  • Streak persistence: streak length distribution (Duo vs. solo)
  • Comfort-level adoption: Quiet vs. Reactions vs. Messages/Voice usage

Key Insight & Next Steps

What I learned: Retention isn’t just a motivation problem — it’s a meaning problem. Users return when progress feels felt, not just tracked. The strongest early moments create a sense of momentum, identity (“I’m someone who does this”), and a reason to show up tomorrow. Social reinforcement can accelerate that by making effort visible and shared.

Next steps: I would test onboarding variations that change the timing and methods of reinforcement (micro-celebrations, streak framing, partner acknowledgement) and measure next-day return, 7-day retention, and streak survival curves. I’d also compare Friend Duo vs. Match Duo to understand which comfort levels drive sustained engagement.

JPMorgan (Harvard Product Lab Client): AI Chatbot for High-Stakes Financial Decisions

Problem & Research

  • Users navigating financial workflows experienced hesitation driven by information overload and low confidence
  • Interviews revealed that uncertainty — not lack of data — was the main barrier to action
  • Many users wanted faster answers but feared making irreversible or costly mistakes

Product Direction

  • Designed an AI chatbot to guide users through complex decisions in a conversational format
  • Focused on clarity, trust calibration, and progressive disclosure rather than automation alone
  • Framed the bot as a “confidence companion,” not just an information retrieval tool

Outcome

  • Reduced hesitation: structured conversations lowered cognitive load
  • Increased clarity: users received contextual explanations instead of static documents
  • Trust-aware design: responses emphasized transparency and clear boundaries

Feature Concept: Confidence-Led Chat

I explored a confidence-led conversation model designed for moments where users feel exposed or uncertain. The goal was not just to provide answers, but to help users feel supported while making high-stakes decisions.

Conversation Design

  • Guided intake: short clarifying prompts to reduce back-and-forth
  • Repair checkpoints: “Did I understand correctly?” moments to prevent misinterpretation
  • Next-step suggestions: structured options instead of open-ended replies
  • Human handoff: seamless escalation with conversation context attached

Trust & Emotional Calibration

  • Confidence signaling: transparent phrasing around certainty levels
  • Source awareness: clarify whether responses are policy-based, account-based, or general guidance
  • Boundary behavior: safe refusals + alternative paths when appropriate
  • Risk-sensitive tone: more cautious and confirmatory language in high-impact scenarios

What I’d Measure

  • Resolution rate: % of conversations ending with user goal completion
  • Containment: successful resolution without human escalation
  • Time-to-resolution: conversation length to clarity
  • Confidence signal: post-chat “felt confident” rating
  • Re-contact rate: repeat inquiries within 7 days

Key Insight & Next Steps

What I learned: In high-stakes contexts, clarity is not enough — users need emotional reassurance. The strongest experiences reduced not only cognitive load, but also the feeling of exposure when making decisions. Designing AI for trust requires visible boundaries, confirmation loops, and tone calibration.

Next steps: I would experiment with varying levels of guidance intensity (minimal vs. structured suggestions) and measure differences in resolution rate, user confidence, and escalation frequency to find the optimal balance between autonomy and support.

Approach

My work centers on understanding user behavior and designing systems that encourage consistent return. I focus on how small interaction details, feedback loops, and moments of progress shape long-term engagement. From research through prototype, I aim to create experiences that feel intentional, motivating, and sustainable over time.
