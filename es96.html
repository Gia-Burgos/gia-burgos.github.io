Individual Work from the First Half of the Semester

Core design process work

Reverse Engineering

Analyzed an existing product by breaking down its structure, function, and user-facing decisions. This helped me better understand how design choices translate into performance, usability, and constraints.

Systems Modeling

Built models to understand how different parts of a system interact. This work strengthened my ability to map relationships, identify bottlenecks, and reason through tradeoffs at a systems level.

Needs Finding

Explored user pain points and contexts through observation, interpretation, and structured design thinking. This stage emphasized designing from real human needs rather than assumptions.

Needs Screening

Compared and prioritized opportunities based on feasibility, relevance, and potential impact. This helped narrow broad observations into clearer directions for product development.

Customer Requirements

Translated user needs into more concrete product requirements. I practiced framing vague or qualitative insights into specific design criteria that could guide decision-making.

Concept Generation & Screening

Generated multiple possible solutions, then evaluated them against user needs and project constraints. This process improved my ability to think expansively first, then critically refine toward stronger concepts.

Skill Building & Training

Methods and tools developed throughout the semester

User-Centered Design

Strengthened my approach to design by grounding ideas in user behavior, needs, and experience rather than only technical functionality.

Idea Evaluation

Practiced comparing concepts using structured criteria such as desirability, feasibility, and alignment with project goals.

Communication & Framing

Developed clearer ways to present design rationale, explain choices, and connect project work to larger product and engineering goals.

Collaboration

Built stronger habits around collaborative problem solving, incorporating feedback, and refining ideas through discussion and iteration.

Team Project Contributions

How course methods carried into collaborative work

From Insights to Design Direction

The early individual assignments directly shaped how I approached the team project. Needs finding, concept generation, and systems thinking gave me a stronger framework for identifying useful opportunities and evaluating possible solutions.

Product Thinking in Practice

In the team setting, I focused on keeping the work aligned with real user value while also considering implementation constraints, iteration speed, and clarity of scope.

Collaboration and Iteration

Working with a team reinforced the importance of balancing diverse ideas, incorporating feedback, and refining concepts through structured discussion and testing.

Reflection

What this work reflects about my goals

This semester deepened my interest in work at the intersection of engineering, systems design, and product development. Across both the individual assignments and team-based projects, I became more intentional about how to identify meaningful problems, translate user needs into concrete requirements, and evaluate ideas with both creativity and rigor.

These experiences align closely with my broader career interests in building thoughtful technical products — especially in environments where I can combine analytical thinking, human-centered design, and end-to-end ownership.
