Circuits

Selected hands-on circuits projects emphasizing clear experimental thinking: defining expected behavior, instrumenting the system, diagnosing mismatches, and communicating results through structured measurements and tradeoffs.

Highlighted Projects

Op-Amps & Audio Signals — Multi-Stage Audio Chain

Designed and built a multi-stage audio chain (input conditioning → active filtering → output amplification) intended to drive a low-impedance speaker load. I validated performance stage-by-stage using oscilloscope measurements and treated debugging as an iterative, evidence-driven process.

Op-Amps AC Coupling Active Filters Breadboard Oscilloscope

How I worked

  • Set expectations first: predicted gain, cutoff behavior, and headroom limits before building.
  • Validated in slices: measured each stage independently to isolate issues early.
  • Debugged systematically: traced distortion/noise back to biasing, coupling, and saturation constraints.
  • Communicated clearly: summarized results as “what we expected vs. what we observed vs. what we changed.”

What I’d optimize next

  • Improve noise performance with grounding/layout discipline and component selection.
  • Increase output stability and reduce clipping under load with better headroom planning.

R-2R DAC + SAR ADC — Arduino + Comparator

Implemented a 4-bit R-2R ladder DAC and paired it with a SAR-style ADC loop using a comparator and Arduino GPIO control. This project focused on mixed-signal integration: translating an algorithmic concept (successive approximation) into measurable, testable electrical behavior.

R-2R Ladder Comparator Arduino GPIO Mixed-Signal

How I worked

  • Designed for observability: built the system so intermediate DAC outputs could be measured during each SAR step.
  • Closed the loop carefully: tested comparator thresholds and timing to avoid “correct code, wrong physics.”
  • Explained behavior: tied waveform shape to quantization, settling time, and threshold sensitivity.
  • Iterated quickly: adjusted resistor tolerance assumptions and sampling timing based on scope evidence.

What I’d optimize next

  • Increase resolution (bits) and analyze error sources (tolerances, offset, noise).
  • Improve settling and stability with buffering and cleaner reference behavior.

RLC Step Response — Under / Critical / Over Damping

Characterized RLC transient behavior by sweeping resistance values and comparing predicted vs. measured step responses. The goal was to build intuition and communicate system dynamics clearly by connecting waveform shape to damping ratio and key parameters.

RLC Transients LTspice Scope Measurement System Damping

How I worked

  • Started from theory: derived ω₀ and ζ, then predicted expected response regimes before testing.
  • Used simulation as a check, not a crutch: compared LTspice outputs against physical measurements.
  • Made results legible: summarized each regime with “parameter change → damping shift → waveform effect.”
  • Handled mismatch honestly: accounted for real-world non-idealities (component tolerance, probe loading).

What I’d optimize next

  • Run repeated trials and report confidence intervals across tolerance ranges.
  • Improve measurement fidelity with careful probing and controlled test conditions.

How I Work (Across Projects)

Make ambiguity measurable

I define expected behavior up front, choose instruments/signals that reveal failure modes, and treat every mismatch as a clue, not a setback.

Communicate clearly

I write and present results in a consistent structure: what we expected → what we observed → what we changed → what we learned. This makes collaboration fast and decisions defensible.

Iterate with evidence

I optimize for quick, testable loops—small changes, targeted measurements, and clear tradeoffs to move from hypothesis to working system efficiently.
