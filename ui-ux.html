UI/UX Case Studies

Mobile interaction design focused on clarity, trust, and emotional reinforcement — grounded in real user feedback.

Rainforest Connection (T4SG) — Live Project

Research

  • Analyzed the conservation app landscape to identify positioning and usability gaps
  • Conducted user interviews to understand engagement barriers and expectations
  • Defined interaction priorities based on observed friction

Design & Validation

  • Designed feedback cues that make impact feel immediate and tangible
  • Prototyped in Figma and iterated based on stakeholder and user feedback
  • Contributed to a cohesive interaction system across core journeys

Impact

  • Clarity: Simplified navigation and reduced interaction friction
  • Emotional connection: Made environmental impact visible through feedback cues
  • Research-grounded: Design decisions reflected real user needs, not assumptions

Key Insight

Users felt more engaged when they could clearly see how their actions connected to real-world impact. Making that connection visible strengthened motivation and return behavior.

Restaurant App — UX Case Study

Research

  • Reviewed dining and reservation platforms to identify common friction patterns
  • Conducted user interviews to observe booking behavior and decision flow
  • Identified a recurring issue: unclear next steps increased hesitation

Design Exploration

  • Designed streamlined flows to clarify progression through the booking process
  • Explored multiple layout and interaction variations in Figma
  • Refined prototypes based on observed usability friction

Outcome

  • Interaction clarity: Made the next action obvious at each step
  • Wayfinding: Reduced ambiguity without removing useful options
  • Behavioral insight: Clear structure builds confidence more than “simplifying” features

Key Insight

Users don’t abandon flows because they are inherently complex — they abandon when they feel uncertain about what to do next.

Feature Concept: Plan Confidence (Dietary-Safe, Socially Easy)

In research, I found that users with dietary restrictions aren’t just navigating a booking flow — they’re navigating trust (is this actually safe?) and social pressure (am I being a burden?). I explored an interaction system that helps users quickly rule out unsafe options, reduce verification work, and participate in group dining with confidence.

User pain points

  • Low trust: vague “we can accommodate” claims don’t address sourcing or cross-contamination
  • Decision fatigue: filtering through ambiguous menus creates stress and extra effort
  • Social stigma: users hesitate to advocate for their needs in group planning
  • Extra verification: calls and external research become the default (often with limited results)

Interaction patterns

  • Dietary Safety Card: clear accommodation details + cross-contamination notes (when available)
  • Confidence labels: “Verified,” “Unclear,” or “Call to confirm” to calibrate trust quickly
  • Smart filtering: prioritize safe matches first; hide clearly incompatible options by default
  • Group-friendly sharing: share 2–3 “safe picks” with a short explanation to reduce awkwardness
  • Reversible decisions: easy edits without restarting the flow (reduces fear of committing)

What I’d measure

  • Time to shortlist: browse → 2–3 viable options (speed + confidence)
  • Verification reduction: fewer calls / external research attempts
  • Completion rate: browse → booked (especially for restricted-diet users)
  • Drop-off points: where uncertainty spikes (e.g., safety details, confirmation)
  • Confidence signal: “felt safe ordering” / “felt comfortable in group” survey

Approach

I design for clarity and return. I start by understanding user behavior, then build interaction systems that reduce friction, calibrate trust, and create reinforcing moments that make people want to come back — especially in socially or emotionally charged flows.
